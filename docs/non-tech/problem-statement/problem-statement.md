# STEP 2 — Problem Discovery

## 1. Problem Context

Bengaluru's rapid urban growth, high vehicle usage, and concentration of residential, commercial, and employment activity have increased pressure on available parking.

In busy areas, parking demand can exceed available capacity. A BBMP infrastructure assessment of Church Street, for example, identified parking shortages and found parking demand to be significantly higher than available capacity. More broadly, Indian urban-transport guidance recognizes parking as a scarce urban resource and emphasizes better management and information about parking availability.

The problem is therefore not only the physical shortage of parking spaces, but also the difficulty of efficiently connecting available parking capacity with people who need it.

## 2. Customer Problem

Drivers looking for parking often face uncertainty about where they can park, whether a space will be available when they arrive, and how much it will cost.

Key pain points include:

- Difficulty finding parking near the destination.
- Spending time driving around searching for a space.
- Limited visibility into parking availability before arrival.
- Uncertainty about pricing and parking duration.
- Risk of reaching a location and finding no suitable parking.
- Reliance on informal or unstructured parking arrangements.

This creates unnecessary time, cost, and frustration for drivers.

## 3. Parking Owner Problem

At the same time, individuals and organizations may have parking spaces that remain unused for part of the day or for extended periods.

Potential providers include homeowners, apartment residents, offices, shops, and other property owners.

Their potential challenges include:

- Difficulty finding trustworthy customers.
- No simple way to publish parking availability.
- Manual coordination of dates and timings.
- Difficulty managing reservations.
- Uncertainty around payments and cancellations.
- Concerns about misuse, security, and disputes.

This creates a potential mismatch between available private parking capacity and nearby parking demand.

## 4. Root Problem

The underlying problem is a **coordination and discoverability gap**.

Parking demand exists at specific locations and times, while parking capacity may exist nearby but is not always easily discoverable, available information is limited, and the transaction is often not structured.

In simple terms:

> **People need parking where and when other people may have unused parking capacity, but there is no simple and trusted way to efficiently connect the two.**

## 5. Problem Statement

**Drivers need a reliable way to discover suitable parking near their destination, while parking owners need a simple and trustworthy way to make unused parking capacity available.**

The product opportunity is to address this gap between parking demand and underutilized parking capacity.

## 6. Initial Problem Hypothesis

Our initial hypothesis is:

> **If drivers can discover relevant parking availability, pricing, and booking options before arriving at their destination, while owners can easily make unused parking capacity available, then parking can be utilized more efficiently and the uncertainty experienced by both sides can be reduced.**

This is an **unvalidated hypothesis**. Further customer interviews, competitor research, and market validation should be used to determine whether the problem is sufficiently important and whether the proposed marketplace model is the right solution.

## 7. Scope of This Discovery

This document establishes the problem we intend to investigate.

It does **not** yet define:

- Detailed product requirements
- Technical architecture
- Microservices
- APIs
- Database design
- Pricing algorithms
- Implementation details

Those decisions will be addressed in the subsequent Business Requirements, Product Requirements, and System Design stages.
