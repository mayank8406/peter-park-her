# STEP 4 — Value Proposition

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this step is to define **why customers and parking owners would choose Peter Park Here over their current alternatives**.

Peter Park Here is a two-sided marketplace, so the value proposition must address both sides of the marketplace.

---

## 2. Customer Value Proposition

### Customer Job

Find a suitable parking space near the destination for the required time, understand the cost, and have confidence that the space will be available.

### Customer Pains

- Time spent searching for parking.
- Uncertainty about whether parking will be available.
- Difficulty comparing nearby options.
- Uncertainty about parking cost.
- Risk of reaching the destination without a suitable space.

### Customer Gains

- Easier discovery of relevant parking options.
- Better visibility into availability and pricing.
- Ability to compare alternatives.
- Greater confidence before travelling.
- A more predictable parking experience.

### Peter Park Here's Value

> **Discover, compare, and reserve suitable parking near your destination with clear availability and pricing, reducing the uncertainty and effort involved in finding parking.**

---

## 3. Parking Owner Value Proposition

### Owner Job

Make unused parking capacity available and generate income without creating excessive operational work.

### Owner Pains

- Unused parking capacity may generate no value.
- Difficulty finding suitable customers.
- Manual coordination of availability and bookings.
- Need to control when the space can be used.
- Concerns around payment, misuse, and disputes.

### Owner Gains

- Generate income from otherwise unused parking capacity.
- Reach potential customers seeking nearby parking.
- Control when the space is available.
- Reduce manual coordination.
- Have greater visibility into bookings and parking usage.

### Peter Park Here's Value

> **Turn unused parking capacity into an income opportunity through a managed marketplace while retaining control over when and how the space is offered.**

---

## 4. Marketplace Value

The two value propositions reinforce each other:

```text
Parking Customers
       │
       │  demand
       ▼
Peter Park Here
       ▲
       │  parking capacity
       │
Parking Owners
```

More useful parking inventory improves customer choice.

More customer demand makes listing parking more valuable to owners.

The fundamental marketplace value is therefore:

> **Connect people who need parking with available parking capacity at the right place and time.**

---

## 5. Differentiation Hypothesis

Peter Park Here should not assume that simply offering parking search and booking is sufficient differentiation. Existing parking services already provide discovery, comparison, advance booking, pricing information, and payment capabilities.

Therefore, our initial differentiation hypothesis should focus on **how well we solve the underlying jobs**, rather than simply matching existing features.

Potential areas to investigate include:

- Reliability of availability information.
- Ease of listing and managing private parking.
- Trust between customers and owners.
- Convenience of the end-to-end experience.
- Efficient use of underutilized private parking capacity.
- Suitability of parking for the customer's specific needs.

These are **hypotheses to validate**, not established competitive advantages.

---

## 6. Value Proposition Summary

### For Customers

> **Find and reserve suitable parking with greater confidence, less searching, and clearer information about availability and cost.**

### For Owners

> **Monetize unused parking capacity with a simple way to control availability and manage customers.**

### For the Marketplace

> **Connect parking demand with underutilized parking capacity and make the exchange more convenient and predictable for both sides.**

---

## 7. Validation Status

The value propositions in this document are **initial hypotheses**.

Evidence that parking discovery, reservation, transparent pricing, and private-space monetization are meaningful product concepts exists in the current market. However, this does not prove that Peter Park Here's specific customer segments will value these propositions enough to adopt or pay for the service.

Customer and owner interviews, competitor analysis, and later product experiments should be used to validate:

- Which jobs matter most.
- Which pains are most significant.
- Which gains customers actually value.
- Whether the proposed value is sufficiently differentiated.
- Whether customers and owners are willing to adopt the marketplace.

---

## 8. Exit Criteria

Step 4 is complete when we can clearly explain:

> **Who receives value, what problem that value addresses, what outcome they gain, and why the proposed value could be preferable to their current alternative.**

The next step is to translate these validated or testable value propositions into **Business Goals and Business Requirements**.
